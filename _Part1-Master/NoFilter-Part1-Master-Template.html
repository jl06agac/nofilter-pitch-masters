The partnership

Coffee in orangutan country

In partnership with international conservation charity People Resources and Conservation Foundation (PRCF), NoFilter sources coffee from smallholder farmers in Tanjung Dolok, Tapanuli: a biodiversity hotspot and home to the critically endangered Tapanuli orangutan.

The partnership

Forests, gibbons & community

NoFilter partners with Fauna & Flora and the Asho Chin Coffee Association (ACCA) to protect watershed forests and western hoolock gibbons through sustainable coffee agroforestry. Sourcing directly supports habitat conservation, local livelihoods, and lasting community impact.

The partnership

In the shadow of the park

Farmers grow coffee in the shadow of Batang Gadis National Park: home to tigers, hornbills, and more biodiversity than you can count on a caffeine high. In partnership with the Sumatra Rainforest Institute (SRI), NoFilter's just getting started here, but true to form, 10% of every sale goes back to protect the forest and support the people who farm with it.

The landscape

Where they all still coexist

Gayo Lues sits at the heart of the Leuser Ecosystem: the only place on Earth where Sumatran orangutans, tigers, elephants and rhinos still share one landscape. Our partner Yayasan Orangutan Sumatera Lestari – Orangutan Information Centre (YOSL-OIC) has worked across Leuser for two decades, helping three communities secure Hutan Desa permits over 2,000 hectares of forest. NoFilter isn't sourcing here yet (the partnership is in scoping) but the groundwork is already in place.

The mechanism

How your cup keeps a forest standing.

Most café coffee hides behind a certification: a logo, a nice-sounding label, a box ticked. But few who buy it (or supply it) could tell you what that stamp actually verifies, who awards it, or whether it holds up. It's trust placed in a name, not a known quantity. At NoFilter we're offering the opposite: giving [[TARGET_NAME]] a direct, traceable stake in NGO-led coffee projects protecting landscapes, and species, too important to lose. Verification we can stand by, and your [[AUDIENCE_PEOPLE]] could one day check for themselves.

  1. 01

    Farmed on the buffer, never across the line

    The coffee comes from growers working the forest's edge, the buffer, alongside conservation NGOs. They grow among the trees and leave the primary forest untouched. Farming that works with the landscape, not against it.

  2. 02

    A hardier crop, a steadier living

    Grown in the shade of the forest, this is a more climate-resilient way to farm than the exposed monocultures coffee usually demands: kinder to the land, and a more reliable income for the growers working it. Because you keep buying, that living holds.

  3. 03

    And then there's the extra layer

    Every purchase does more than pay fairly for the coffee. On top of that, 10% of our gross profit goes straight back to the same NGOs and farming partners on the ground, helping them hold the line and future-proof the coffee operations these forests depend on. They know how to do the work; your coffee is what keeps it funded.

Verified, not claimed

Proof, not promises

Anyone can claim their coffee protects forests. Almost no one can show you. Every plot we buy from is mapped against official conservation boundaries: government forestry data, held and checked by the NGOs on the ground. The line of what's protected isn't ours to move. Some layers are live, others are still rolling out. But the aim is simple: not a promise to trust, a record you can check.

Layer 01 · Live

Every plot, drawn as data

Each coffee plot, its buffer, and the primary forest beyond are mapped as GPS boundaries — partner-signed and held as data. The line between farmed and untouched isn't a claim. It's a coordinate.

Status · Live across sourced origins
Layer 02 · Live

Watched from orbit, by others

Every polygon is enrolled in Global Forest Watch: weekly radar and optical passes that flag canopy change independently, run by an open monitoring system with no stake in what we source. This map is live: pan and zoom the real evidence.

Status · Live · external by design
Layer 03 · Rolling out

A record from above, year on year

Once a year, each landscape is photographed from the air along the same path, building a high-resolution picture of the forest canopy we can compare season to season: proof, over time, that it is still standing. Rolling out across our origins through 2026.

Status · Rolling out · 2026
Layer 04 · Rolling out

Boots on the ground, on the record

Quarterly photographs, GPS-tagged and taken by our NGO partners on the ground (PRCF, SRI, YOSL-OIC and Fauna & Flora), confirming what the data shows from above. The human layer that closes the loop. Rolling out across our origins through 2026.
